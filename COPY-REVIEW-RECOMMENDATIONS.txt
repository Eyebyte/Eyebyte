EYEBYTE — COPY & CONTENT REVIEW
==============================

Purpose
-------
Make the website easier to scan, more specific, less repetitive and more persuasive
without losing Eyebyte's confident live-production voice. The suggestions below are
editorial recommendations, not claims that have been independently verified.

WHAT IS ALREADY WORKING
-----------------------
- “Live Visuals. Built to Impress.” is direct, memorable and visually strong. Keep it.
- “Performance. Hardware. Content.” gives visitors a clear mental model of Eyebyte.
- “More than a rectangle.” is an effective differentiator for custom modular LED.
- “Got a Big Idea? Good.” has personality and is a strong primary contact headline.
- “From the first sketch. To the Last beat.” gives the process a clear beginning and end.
- The three dedicated service pages are a better search and reading structure than one
  long homepage trying to explain every service.

HIGHEST-VALUE COPY IMPROVEMENTS
-------------------------------

1. HERO — make the proposition more concrete
Current: “Eyebyte brings live video performance, custom LED environments and inventive
thinking to stages that deserve more than just a rectangular screen.”
Suggested: “Eyebyte brings live VJ performance, custom modular LED and original visual
content together—designed around the stage, the music and the audience.”
Why: It names the three services in language customers actually use, then explains the
benefit. “Inventive thinking” is positive but abstract; “original visual content” is clearer.

2. THREE PILLARS — remove repeated wording
Live Performance suggested: “Live visuals mixed and shaped in real time—responding to
the music, the energy in the room and the moment. Not just playback. Performance.”
Hardware suggested: “Custom modular LED systems, including true flexible curved panels,
configured around the stage design, sightlines and event.”
Content suggested: “Bespoke event visuals, a deep Eyebyte library and real-time effects,
combined into a visual identity built for the screen and the show.”
Why: Each card should explain a different reason to choose Eyebyte, not repeat “real-time”,
“bespoke” and “event” in all three descriptions.

3. EXPERIENCE — keep the claim, make the benefit clearer
Current idea: Eyebyte has thrived since 1995, delivering thousands of events and
entertaining millions.
Suggested: “Since 1995, Eyebyte has delivered thousands of events and created visual
experiences for millions. Three decades of live production bring hard-won judgement to
every brief—from the first design decision to the final show cue.”
Why: It connects longevity to what a client receives. Only retain “thousands” and “millions”
if you are comfortable substantiating those claims when asked.

4. PROCESS — simplify the descriptions and make each stage distinct
Start with the vision: “We bring creative and production teams together early, aligning
the stage, screen system and visual direction before the build begins.”
Design the Spatial Experience: “We shape screen layouts, content and playback around
the space, sightlines and creative brief. Every pixel and every form has a purpose.”
Make it happen: “On site, we work hands-on with the hardware, coordinate the crew and
solve problems as they happen. Good planning keeps delivery on time and on budget—
without compromising the idea.”
Mastery in the Moment: “Live visual performance, precise timing and real-time tools bring
the system to life—responding to the music, the crowd and the moment. Precision when it
matters. Instinct when it counts.”
Why: Each stage now has one job: align, design, deliver, perform. Avoid repeating
“real-time” in every stage.

5. HARDWARE — replace broad superlatives with proof
Current copy uses phrases such as “exceptional resolution”, “vibrant brightness” and
“high refresh rates”. They sound good but do not help a technical buyer compare systems.
Recommended improvements:
- Publish verified pixel pitch / native resolution information.
- State measured brightness in nits, refresh rate in Hz, contrast information if available,
  and the exact IP rating.
- Explain what “true flexible curved panels” enable in practical design terms.
- Clarify panel sizes, system dimensions, outdoor suitability, control and camera integration.
- Add a short specification table or downloadable datasheet.
Do not publish a number unless it matches the actual panel and operating configuration.

Suggested lead: “Beyond the boundaries of a conventional flat wall. Eyebyte's modular
LED system creates custom screen formats—including true flexible curves—around the
stage design. The result is a screen system shaped for the space, not the other way around.”

6. IN THE FIELD — present relationships as selected work
Current: “From enduring festival relationships to bespoke corporate activations, this is
a snapshot of Eyebyte's recent favourite event brands.”
Suggested: “Selected festival partnerships and live projects, built through years of visual
direction, VJ coordination and show delivery.”
Why: “Favourite event brands” is informal and unclear; “selected partnerships” sounds
more considered. Keep the role and year attached to each event so the relationship is clear.

For each project, use a consistent format:
EVENT NAME
Eyebyte role · Since YYYY
One short sentence describing the work, if you can substantiate it.

7. CONTACT — make the next step feel easy
Current: “Tell us what you're imagining, what the space looks like, and what you want
people to feel. We'll work from there.”
Suggested: “Tell us about the event, the space and the experience you want to create.
We'll help shape the visual system to make it happen.”
Why: Keeps the friendly tone but is more direct and action-oriented.

8. SERVICE PAGES — reduce repetition and deepen useful detail
The three service pages currently share a similar structure and several repeated phrases
(“What this brings to your event”, “Part of the same system” and the same contact copy).
Keep the shared design, but give each page a genuinely different middle section:
- Live VJ: live mixing, music response, timecode, VJ coordination, show-day workflow.
- LED hardware: panel formats, flexible curves, resolution, brightness, refresh, IP rating,
  staging and control integration.
- Content: custom content pipeline, visual library, unusual screen ratios, real-time effects,
  playback formats and deliverables.
Add real examples and FAQs specific to the service, not boilerplate questions.

9. READABILITY — apply these rules throughout
- Keep most paragraphs to 2–3 sentences.
- Aim for one idea per paragraph.
- Use short subheadings that describe the benefit, not vague labels.
- Keep a consistent capitalisation style: sentence case for body headings unless a deliberate
  display headline is used. Avoid inconsistent Title Case.
- Explain specialist terms once (for example, timecoded performance).
- Replace generic adjectives (“exceptional”, “uncompromising”, “unique”) with a fact, example
  or result wherever possible.
- Keep button labels action-led: “Explore the work”, “Open Screen Builder”, “Start a project”.

10. PROOF TO ADD
- 3–5 detailed case studies with original photos and video.
- One or two short client testimonials with permission.
- Actual technical LED specifications and real-world application examples.
- A clear statement of service area (Sydney-based; available across Australia, if accurate).
- Credits and links from event partners where permission and attribution are agreed.

IMPORTANT
---------
These are suggestions for the next copy edit. No unverified client names, event outcomes,
technical specifications, audience totals or performance claims have been added to the site.
